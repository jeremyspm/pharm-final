The seven terms

TermDefinitionExample
LicitLawful and permitted: prescription, restricted, pharmacy-only, general sale medicines + some Class B & C controlled drugsAlcohol; an opioid taken as prescribed
IllicitUnlawful / not permitted: all Class A, some Class B, a few Class CMethamphetamine (Class A)
MisuseAny substance used for a purpose different from legal or medical guidelines (WHO). NZ law: illegal, harmful or unauthorised use of a controlled drug (Misuse of Drugs Act 1975)Taking someone else's prescription
AbuseSelf-administered in chronically excessive amounts, outside approved medical use or social norms, causing physical or psychological harmHeavy daily drinking that damages health
DependencePursued compulsively, no medical need, despite adverse effects. Psychological: out-of-control cravings, often denial. Physical: withdrawal when not usedOpioid stopped suddenly → withdrawal (so wean). Insulin, daily inhaler = medical dependence, not addiction
AddictionA behavioural pattern: overwhelming involvement in getting and using; impairs motivation-reward, mood, behavioural inhibition; high relapseKeeps using despite losing job; also gambling, eating, shopping
ToleranceRepeated doses → decreasing effect, or dose must rise for the same effect (receptor downregulation)Morphine: rapid. Marijuana: little

Withdrawal & substance use disorder P

Withdrawal: mild → severe, days–weeks; depends on the substance, how long, age/health, weaned vs "cold turkey". E.g. insomnia, fatigue, irritability, cravings; severe: paranoia, confusion, tremors.

Substance use disorder: diagnosed with the DSM-5; severity = number of criteria met (11 criteria, incl. cravings, tolerance, withdrawal). Mild 2–3 · moderate 4–5 · severe 6+.

Risk factors (young adults) · who uses

F-I-S-B: Family (relatives using, home stress, childhood trauma) · Individual (impulsive, mental ill health, access e.g. Rx after injury) · Social & environmental (peers, societal acceptance, laws) · Behavioural (frequent use, starting "socially"). Anyone can take drugs: avoid stereotyping.

NZ picture P: most adults drink, ~1 in 6 hazardously; daily vaping now above smoking; cannabis the most-used illicit drug; MDMA, cocaine, opioids rising (NZ Health Survey 2024/25, Drug Foundation 2025, Youth19).

Stimulant or depressant? P

Stimulants ("uppers") speed up the CNS: MDMA, methamphetamine. Depressants ("downers") slow it: alcohol, GHB, sedatives (lorazepam, zopiclone), nitrous oxide, inhalants. Both: nicotine, marijuana, psychedelics, synthetic cannabinoids. Slang: P = meth · 420 = marijuana · lean = codeine + fizzy drink · pingers = MDMA · fishies = GHB · nangs = nitrous · acid = LSD.

Interactions

Medication–drug: a licit/illicit substance changes a medicine's effect → check the NZ Formulary Interactions checker (it lists alcohol, marijuana, nicotine, sedatives, opioids). E.g. alcohol + another depressant (opioid, benzodiazepine) → overdose risk; 35% of NZ fatal overdoses involved alcohol.

Drug–drug P: Know Your Stuff combination chart, a harm-reduction chance. Caution: alcohol + nitrous; caffeine + amphetamines. Dangerous: alcohol + ketamine; opioids + cocaine; SSRI + tramadol.

Nurse's role · referral · harm reduction

Ask about use · brief intervention · offer referrals · follow up · advocate. Consider mental health services too.

Services: Community Alcohol & Drug Services (CADS) · Te Ātea Marinō (Māori) · Tupu (Pasifika) · Altered High (youth) · rehab e.g. Odyssey House.

Websites: DrugFoundation.org.nz · TheLevel.org.nz · AlcoholDrugHelp.org.

Harm reduction: make use safer for people who'll use anyway: legal, free drug checking (Know Your Stuff NZ; over half won't take a drug that isn't what they thought), naloxone for people at risk of opioid overdose.

Toxicology: poisoning & overdose

Poisoning: ingestion or contact (heavy metals, pesticides, household cleaners), usually accidental. Acute = enters in a short time · Chronic = gradual build-up. Most things can poison, depending on how much, age & size, already unwell, other medicines.

Poisonous = you touch/eat it (plant, berry, mushroom). Venomous = it bites/stings you (bee, wasp, katipō, white-tailed spider, bluebottle).

National Poisons Centre 0800 764 766 (0800 POISON) · free · 24 h. Swallowed medicine/product/chemical/plant · overdose · inhaled fumes · splash on eyes/skin · symptoms from recreational drugs. Unconscious or not breathing → 111.

Antidotes

Counteract a poison by: binding it (activated charcoal) · enzyme inhibition · enzyme activation · blocking receptors (antagonists).

Naloxone (Narcan): opioid antagonist, reverses opioid overdose. N-acetylcysteine (NAC): reduces the toxic metabolites of paracetamol.
